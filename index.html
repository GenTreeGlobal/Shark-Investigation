That 100-million number isn't just a cartoon line — it's the real, scientifically-estimated figure. The case files below dig into exactly why it happens, and what real "creature powers" people use to stop it.

Case File 01

Why sharks matter

Sharks are apex predators — the ocean's clean-up crew and referees. They keep fish populations balanced and coral reefs and seagrass beds healthy. When sharks disappear, the whole ocean food web starts to wobble.

Case File 02

The threats sharks are facing

Sharks grow slowly, mature late, and have very few pups — so when too many are killed, populations can't bounce back quickly. Here's what's putting them in danger.

Shark finning

Sharks are caught only for their fins — used in shark fin soup — and the rest of the body is often thrown back. It's the single biggest reason shark numbers are dropping.

Biggest threat

Bycatch

Huge fishing nets and long lines set for tuna or shrimp accidentally trap sharks too. Most were never the target — they're caught by mistake.

Accidental

Habitat loss

Coastal development, damaged coral reefs, and warming water destroy the nursery grounds baby sharks need to grow up safely.

Growing risk

Slow to recover

Some sharks take 15+ years to have their first pup, and have only a handful at a time. Losing adults faster than they can reproduce pushes species toward extinction.

Biology works against them
Case File 03

Creature powers: how people help sharks

Just like the team activates a creature power to solve a rescue, here's the real-world "powers" scientists, governments, and everyday people use to protect sharks.

1

Sanctuaries & protected waters

Countries create shark sanctuaries and Marine Protected Areas where fishing for sharks is banned entirely, giving populations a safe place to recover.

2

Finning bans & fishing limits

Laws that require sharks to be landed with fins still attached, and catch limits on threatened species, make illegal finning much harder to hide.

3

Bycatch-safe fishing gear

Circle hooks, escape hatches in nets, and "shark-safe" fishing lines let accidentally-caught sharks swim free instead of dying on the line.

4

Choosing sustainable seafood

Families can pick seafood certified sustainable (and skip shark meat, cartilage pills, or fin products), which lowers demand that drives overfishing.

5

Shark science & tracking

Tagging and tracking sharks tells scientists where they feed and raise their young, so protections can go exactly where sharks need them most.

6

Telling everyone why sharks matter

Fear sells shark products; understanding protects sharks. Documentaries, aquariums, and kids teaching other kids all shrink the demand that hurts sharks.

Quick shark facts

Older than treesSharks first appeared over 400 million years ago, before trees existed on Earth.
Rays are familyRays and chimaeras are shark relatives — and many are in even more danger than sharks.
Keystone predatorsRemoving sharks can cause smaller predator numbers to explode, throwing off the whole food chain.
